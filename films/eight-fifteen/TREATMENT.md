# Eight Fifteen — treatment

A ~2-minute narrated film about the atomic bombings of Hiroshima (6 Aug 1945) and Nagasaki (9 Aug 1945), drawn in the style of the user's reference still life: flat cel fills, thin warm-brown ink outlines, muted dusty teal / sand / brick-red palette, soft cel shadow, paper grain. Sources on screen at the end.

## Style (from the user's reference image)

- **Look:** quiet still life. Flat colour fills, one shadow tone per object, a single soft highlight. Line: thin, dark warm brown (`#2e211d`), round caps, slight hand wobble that boils on twos (12 fps). Background walls are mottled, painted, never flat.
- **Palette:** wall teal `#9bb8b9`, sand / vase `#d8a77f`, table cream `#e9dcc2`, brick red `#b8434a`, wine `#3a1f22`, mauve `#8c5c66`, blossom cream `#f6efe6`. Aftermath palette: ash `#b9b2a6`, ochre smoke `#9b8566`, charcoal `#4a403a`.
- **Type:** Shippori Mincho (OFL): a Japanese-designed serif with a calm, printed feel. Subtitles in cream with a soft dark band, lower third.
- **Motion:** slow. Camera smooth every frame; drawings boil on twos.
- **Tone:** restraint. No bodies, no gore. Loss is told through objects, absence and silence.

## Three candidate structures

1. **Countdown timeline** — dated cards, 6 Aug → 9 Aug → 15 Aug → today. Clear, but a slideshow; nothing to feel.
2. **One survivor's day** — follow an invented child through the morning. Moving, but it invents a person in a real atrocity.
3. **The still life that stops** — one table, one window, one pocket watch. The world outside changes; the table remembers. The watch stops at 8:15; the film returns to the same table at the end, with cranes and oleander. **Chosen**: it lets the reference image's genre (still life) carry the story, keeps every human detail factual, and gives a bookend.

## Logline and arc

A pocket watch on a table in Hiroshima ticks toward 8:15; after two cities are destroyed, the same table holds paper cranes and the oleander that bloomed in the ashes.

Setup (morning, ordinary life) → turn (8:15 — colour leaves the world; 11:02 — again) → ending (survivors' memory: cranes, oleander, the annual silence).

## Benchmark

- *Grave of the Fireflies* (1988) and *In This Corner of the World* (2016): **learn** the restraint, the still domestic objects that hold the grief, the long holds and the silence before catastrophe. **Don't take** characters, designs, shots or music.
- Still-life painting (Morandi): **learn** the patience of objects on a table and the low-saturation harmony.

## Native move

**The colour leaves the drawing.** At the instant of each detonation the frame flashes white and the flat fills vanish, leaving only the ink outlines — as if the world were burned down to its drawing. It is used twice (Hiroshima, then faster and quieter for Nagasaki). At the end colour returns, first in the cranes, then in the oleander.

## Cue map

Free-time score (no click), in a Japanese *in* pentatonic (D–E♭–G–A–B♭) on a plucked koto-like string (Karplus–Strong, `pluck.py`) over a soft synthesized drone. ~60 bpm feel, phrases every 4 s.
- 0–24: sparse koto phrases over a warm D drone; cicada bed. Stops dead at 24.0.
- 24–28: no music. Ticking, then nothing.
- 28–45: low drone only, darker (D + E♭ cluster, filtered), rain and wind.
- 45–63: drone; a single repeated low note; second flash at 56.5 with no music for 1.5 s.
- 63–74: radio crackle bed; music silent from 71.8 (silence 2).
- 74–97: koto returns, gentle, one note per crane; rises in register as cranes fly (92–97).
- 97–113.5: bell at 101.5; koto phrase resolves on D; held drone fades.
- 113.5–134.5: quiet drone, fades out by 134.
