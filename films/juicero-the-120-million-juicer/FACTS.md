## Wording differences between the narration and the sources (narration kept verbatim)

- **"In March 2016" (S04):** Fortune says "spring of 2016" and, in September 2017, "16 months ago". Wikipedia gives March 2016; a Juicero press release and BevNET give April. The screen says "spring 2016" and notes the discrepancy. "Approximately seventeen months after launch" (S14) fits a March–April launch.
- **"$120 million":** Bloomberg (Apr 2017) says "about $120 million", Bloomberg (Sep 2017) "more than $100 million", Crunchbase via FoodNavigator $118.5M. The screen says it is capital raised, not money lost, and that no single investor is reported to have put in $120 million.
- **"Campbell Soup":** named by Crunchbase via FoodNavigator, not by Bloomberg.
- **"helped protect freshness" (S06):** per a person close to the company, the QR check stops expired or recalled packs; Dunn cited app reminders before packs expire.
- **Teardown (S07):** Ars Technica's summary of a third-party teardown. The film shows a 2018 photo of a disassembled press at Bolt and gives no cost or specification.
- **"less than two hundred dollars" (S13):** Ars says "under $200"; Fortune's source says "in the $200 range". Shown as a reported plan that never launched.
- **S12:** the supply-chain sequence is labelled illustrative and tied to the company's own shutdown letter.
