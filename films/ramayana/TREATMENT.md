# The Seven Books — a pop-up Ramayana (treatment)

~2:00 (121 s), 1920×1080, 30 fps. A paper pop-up book on a real wooden desk tells Valmiki's Ramayana with cut-paper **stick figures**.
Style: `styles/paper-popup/STYLE.md` (kept whole). Narration: one warm storyteller (Kokoro TTS, offline).
Sources for the story: Valmiki's Ramayana (seven kandas). Kept to Valmiki: no later additions such as the
Lakshmana rekha; the Uttara Kanda is flagged as widely considered a later addition.

## Three candidate structures

1. **Hero's journey, one long road** — Rama walks a single strip that unrolls across many pages. Clean, but it flattens the epic's own architecture.
2. **Two books facing each other** — Ayodhya on one, Lanka on the other, characters cross the gap. Strong for the war, weak for exile and the forest.
3. **The almanac** — one spread per stage of the poem, each with its own palette and one pull-tab "trick"; a hanging sign names the books on each spread, and a page turn is a chapter change. **Chosen**: the pop-up book's physical structure mirrors the poem's seven books. At two minutes, the seven books are paired onto four spreads (1–2, 3, 4–5, 6–7) after a prologue.

Opening from the topic: **the poem's own origin** — before the book has colour, a graphite spread shows Valmiki at the river Tamasa; a hunter's arrow, the krauncha bird's cry, grief turning into the first verse. The verse's ink then colours the cover: the book "is written". Ending: **one piece stays up** — the book folds closed, chapter by chapter, but the card of Valmiki and the twins Lava and Kusha singing stays standing, then the cover closes around… no: it stays outside on the desk, small, singing, as the camera cranes up.

## Logline / arc

How grief by a river became a poem in seven books: birth and the bow → exile → abduction → alliance → the leap → war and return → the poem comes home to its author.

## Benchmark (grammar only)

- Pop-up books by master paper engineers: layered V-folds, rising back to front, pull-tabs. **Learn**: mechanism as event. **Don't take**: designs.
- Indian picture-scroll storytelling (*pattachitra*, *kaavad*): a narrator opening panels one by one. **Learn**: sequence of panels, a storyteller's cadence. **Don't take**: specific motifs.

## Characters (stick figures in cut paper)

Thick dark-brown ink line bodies, round heads, one colour garment shape, white cut border. Identity by colour + one prop:
Valmiki (white beard, ochre robe) · Rama (blue head, bow) · Sita (saffron-red sari shape) · Lakshmana (gold, bow) ·
Dasharatha (crown, grey beard) · Kaikeyi (purple) · Bharata (green, carries sandals) · Hanuman (orange, tail, mace) ·
Sugriva (brown monkey) · Ravana (ten small heads in a row, dark green) · Jatayu (paper bird) · Lava & Kusha (two small figures).

## Camera grammar used

Descending dolly from human height into the opening; eye-level macro tracks for walks; pull-ups to 3/4 for each page turn; push-ins on faces at the decisions (Rama accepting exile, Sita taken, Hanuman's ring); a reverse shot into the blurred room as the book opens; rising crane at the end.

## Sound

Paper foley as signature (page swish, pop snaps, slat flaps, tab slides, taps). Music box + pizzicato + soft tanpura-like drone (a gentle Indian colour on toy instruments, no imitation of devotional melodies). Room tone + clock in the real world; one bed per spread (river, city, forest crickets, wind on hills, sea, drums of war far off, hermitage birds). Silences before the abduction and before the closing.
