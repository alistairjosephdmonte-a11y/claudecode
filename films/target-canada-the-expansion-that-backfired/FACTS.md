# Facts and sources: Brand Coroner · Case 014 · Target Canada

Editorial rule: every historical claim is supported by documentary evidence. Target Corporation's own press releases and SEC filings come first, then contemporary Canadian reporting and the Canadian Business investigation. Quotations on screen are typeset transcriptions labelled "not a facsimile".

**Main sources**

- **Target release, Jan 13, 2011**: "Target Corporation to Acquire Interest in Canadian Real Estate from Zellers Inc., a Subsidiary of Hudson's Bay Company, for C$1.825 Billion" (corporate.target.com).
- **Target release, Jan 15, 2015**: "Target Corporation Announces Plans to Discontinue Canadian Operations" (corporate.target.com).
- **Form 10-K, fiscal 2013**: year ended Feb 1, 2014, filed Mar 14, 2014 (SEC EDGAR, tgt-20140201x10k.htm).
- **Form 10-K, fiscal 2014**: year ended Jan 31, 2015, filed Mar 13, 2015 (SEC EDGAR, tgt-20150131x10k.htm).
- **Canadian Business**: Joe Castaldo, "The Last Days of Target: The untold tale of Target Canada's difficult birth, tough life and brutal death" (Jan 2016). Based on interviews with former employees.
- **CBC News, Aug 19, 2013**: "Target failing to impress customers in Canada" (Forum Research survey).
- **The Associated Press, Apr 7, 2014**: Anne D'Innocenzio and Charmaine Noronha, "U.S. retailers getting chilly reception in Canada" (via Global News).
- **Reuters, May 8, 2014**: Andrea Hopkins, "Factbox: Target Canada prices versus U.S. Target prices" (via Fox Business).
- **MPR News, Jul 31, 2014**: "Target hires PepsiCo executive Brian Cornell as new CEO".

## Storyboard items not used

- **Logos as graphics.** No Target, Zellers or Hudson's Bay logo is reproduced as a design element. Signage appears only within dated photographs of real stores. Two candidate photographs were rejected because a logo dominated the frame: an illuminated bullseye entrance (Edmonton, 2014) and a Starbucks counter (Brampton, 2013).
- **Price tags.** The storyboard's "$24.99 vs $34.99" tags are not real figures. The film shows the documented statements about prices (Reuters, AP) and says on screen that no matched, dated item comparison is shown.
- **The Cornell quote.** The storyboard's "We do not see a realistic path to profitability before 2021" paraphrases the release. The film uses the exact wording: "unable to find a realistic scenario that would get Target Canada to profitability until at least 2021."
- **"$1.0B USD (first year)".** Replaced with the 10-K figure, −US$941 million segment EBIT, with the recast and pre-opening figures noted on screen.
- **Executive portraits.** No freely licensed photograph of Gregg Steinhafel could be verified, so none is shown, and the film says so on screen. Brian Cornell appears in a licensed 2024 photograph, labelled with its date. No staged executives, shoppers or warehouse workers.
- **The inventory-system screen and warehouse-flow graphic.** The storyboard's "Errors / Incomplete / Inaccurate" screen is not reproduced as if it were real software. The product-record panel lists only the field types Canadian Business names, with no values, and is labelled illustrative. The system diagram names the three systems Canadian Business identifies.
- **Map.** In place of an animated store map, Scene 04 shows the 10-K's store count by province.
